---
title: "8.1 The two formats"
---

Companies use "LLD round" to mean two quite different things. Find out which one you're facing before the day. Ask the recruiter directly; they will usually tell you.

## 8.1.1 Whiteboard / discussion LLD (45–60 minutes)

You get a prompt such as "Design BookMyShow" or "Design a parking lot". You talk through requirements, draw a class diagram (on a whiteboard, Excalidraw or a shared doc), name the key interfaces, walk through one or two flows with a sequence diagram, and maybe write one or two important classes or methods. Nothing has to compile. The interviewer is judging your **modelling**: the right entities, clean responsibilities, sensible relationships, patterns used for a reason, and how you respond to "what if…?".

Amazon is the classic example. Write-ups describe a discussion round with class, state and sequence diagrams, sometimes followed by coding one class or function. Angel One's SDE-2 loop has a dedicated LLD round too, though public detail is thin (more on that below).

## 8.1.2 Machine coding (90–150 minutes)

You get a written spec, often a page or two long, and you must build a **working, in-memory, console-driven application** in your own IDE. No database, no framework, no UI. At the end you demo it (usually with a driver/`main` file or tests) and then an engineer reviews the code with you for 15–30 minutes, asking you to justify design choices and extend the solution on the spot.

Flipkart made this format famous, and many Indian product companies copied it: PhonePe, Razorpay, Groww, Swiggy, Meesho, CRED, Uber. The key difference from a whiteboard round is that **working code is the price of entry**. A beautiful design with nothing running is usually a reject. The one exception reported publicly is a Flipkart candidate who passed with incomplete code because the design was strong, and you shouldn't plan around being that person.

| Aspect | Whiteboard / discussion LLD | Machine coding |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 45–60 min | 90–150 min, plus 15–30 min review |
| Output | Diagrams, interfaces, 1–2 classes | Complete runnable app + demo |
| Tooling | Whiteboard, doc, Excalidraw | Your IDE (or HackerRank/CodeSignal) |
| Scored on | Modelling, relationships, patterns, trade-offs | Working code, modularity, extensibility, validation, tests, speed |
| Biggest risk | Staying vague, drawing boxes without behaviour | Running out of time with nothing that runs |
| Typical companies | Amazon, Angel One (LLD round), senior rounds everywhere | Flipkart, PhonePe, Razorpay, Groww, Swiggy, Meesho, CRED, Uber, Atlassian |

> **Angel One specifically.** Public data is thin and every detail comes from search snippets, not full posts. Prepare for both formats. Practise at least one trading-domain problem (order book or matching engine, watchlist with price alerts, portfolio/P&L, leaderboard) in *both* the 60-minute discussion style and the 90-minute coding style. The concurrency discussion in this chapter matters there: two orders hitting the same balance is the stockbroker version of "two people borrowing the last copy".
