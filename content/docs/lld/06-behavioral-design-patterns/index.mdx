---
title: "6. Behavioral Design Patterns"
---

Creational patterns decide *how objects are born*. Structural patterns decide *how objects are wired together*. Behavioral patterns answer a third question: **who does what, who talks to whom, and who decides what happens next?** Most LLD interview problems are really about behavior. A parking lot needs a pricing rule, a vending machine needs states, a stock app needs alerts, and a text editor needs undo. So this chapter will probably pay off the most in your interviews.

**What you'll learn**

- The 12 classic behavioral patterns (Strategy, Observer, Command, State, Chain of Responsibility, Template Method, Iterator, Mediator, Memento, Visitor, Null Object, Interpreter), each shown with realistic broker and trading code in TypeScript.
- The TypeScript way to write each one: typed events, discriminated unions, generators, async iterators, `WeakMap`-backed mementos, and functions used as strategies.
- How to tell look-alike patterns apart: Strategy vs State, Command vs Strategy, Observer vs Mediator vs Pub/Sub, Visitor vs `switch` on a union.
- Which LLD interview problems each pattern fits, and how to explain your choice in one or two sentences.
- A comparison table and a "pattern picker" cheat sheet to revise the night before the interview.

> **Mentor note.** Don't try to memorize the UML. For each pattern, learn **the one question it answers**. Strategy answers "how should this step be done?", State answers "what am I allowed to do right now?", and Observer answers "who needs to know?". If you can say that question out loud in an interview, the rest of the explanation follows.
